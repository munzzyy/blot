The famous redaction failures all worked the same way: a black box drawn
over text that was still there underneath.

Blot cannot make that mistake, because it does not keep the text:

* Every page becomes a picture; your ink becomes part of the picture.
* A brand new PDF is built containing only those pictures. The writer has
  no code paths for text, forms, annotations, or metadata, so nothing
  hidden in the input can ride along.
* The finished file is reopened and re-checked in front of you: zero
  extractable text, zero annotations, zero form fields.
* Documents that cannot be flattened honestly (password-protected, filled
  forms, XFA, signed) are refused with an explanation and advice.

The output is a picture of a document: prints and reads fine, not
editable or searchable. That is the price of certain.

This app requests no permissions and the OS refuses every network
connection. One pinned, checksum-verified dependency (Mozilla's PDF.js)
reads the input; everything else is dependency-free. MIT licensed.
